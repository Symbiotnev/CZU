01 — The Problem
01 — The problem

Land is the bottleneck. Appetite was never the problem.

Mbuziplus already works — two goats, a zero-grazing unit, a proven husbandry model. But it stops at the fence of whoever owns land. That quietly locks out the exact people most able to fund it.

The urban congregant

Has capital and interest, no homestead. Currently has no way into livestock ownership at all.

The diaspora member

Wants a stake back home. No one on the ground to manage a unit for them.

The willing farmer

Ready to do the work, but a single homestead can't absorb the capital or the scale on its own.

02 — The Model
02 — The model

One piece of church land. Many owners.

We lease a central plot — ideally PCEA church land — and build one shared zero-grazing enterprise on it. The church contributes land, members contribute capital, Mbuziplus contributes the livestock, housing and husbandry systems.

Church

Land & standing

Leases the site, lends its trust network to recruit the cohort.

Members

Capital & labour

20–30 congregants fund entry and share the running costs.

Mbuziplus

Livestock & systems

Supplies goats, housing, husbandry protocol and offtake links.

Together: one Communal Zero-Grazing Unit (CZU) — a single enterprise, jointly owned, run to a 5-year growth plan.

03 — Membership
03 — Membership

Built for the farmer, and for the farmer at heart.

Not every congregant wants to show up and feed goats — and not every one can. Two tiers let the CZU absorb both kinds of member without diluting either.

Tier one

On-site member

  • Lives near the unit, contributes labour toward feed, security and upkeep
  • Reduced cash contribution in exchange for hands-on work
  • First in line for napier and fodder-growing income
Tier two

Investor member

  • Urban or diaspora — capital only, zero labour required
  • Full pooled cash contribution, fully passive
  • Quarterly reporting on their unit — like a SACCO statement, for goats

07 — Scalability
07 — Scalability

Built to add a new cohort every year.

The pilot proves the model at 20–30 members on one plot. Each following year adds a fresh cohort onto the same site's spare capacity, then to additional PCEA land as the church signs on.

08 — Governance
08 — Governance & protection

How your money — and the church's name — are protected.

Formal legal wrapper

Registered as an investment club / SACCO-style entity, with the church as a named land partner.

Ring-fenced funds

Member contributions held by a named custodian, separate from any individual's control.

Insurance from year two

Annual premium covers herd loss once the pilot's first debts are serviced.

Defined lease term

Land agreement with the church set for a minimum five years, with renewal terms agreed upfront.

Quarterly reporting

Every member — on-site or investor — receives a statement on their unit's health and output.

Defined exit terms

Clear process and timing for a member who needs to exit before the five-year horizon.

10 — The Ask
10 — The ask

What we're asking of PCEA.

1

Lease of a central plot

Church retains ownership throughout; a defined lease term and a share of enterprise revenue return to PCEA.

2

Endorsement to the congregation

Support in recruiting the first cohort of 20–30 members from within PCEA's own trust network.

3

A seat in governance

PCEA holds a formal role overseeing the enterprise, protecting both its name and its members' interests.

Pilot phase runs a minimum of five years, then replicates to other PCEA congregations using the same structure.
