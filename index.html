Communal Zero-Grazing Units

Imfuyo Mbuziplus

One plot of land. Many owners. Five years of compounding growth.

Mbuziplus already works — two goats, one zero-grazing unit, a proven husbandry model. A Communal Zero-Grazing Unit (CZU) takes that same model and lets a whole congregation own it together, whether or not any single member has land of their own.

Pilot cohort20–30 members
Minimum horizon5 years

What is a CZU

Land is the bottleneck. Appetite was never the problem.

The standard Mbuziplus model stops at the fence of whoever owns land. A CZU removes that fence — one shared enterprise, built on a single leased plot, open to anyone who wants in.

Urban congregant

Capital, no homestead

Has the means and the interest but nowhere to keep livestock. A CZU gives them a stake without a farm.

Diaspora member

A stake back home

Wants ownership in Kenya but has no one on the ground to manage a single unit for them.

Willing farmer

Work, at real scale

Ready to do the hands-on work, but a single homestead can't absorb enough capital to grow fast alone.

How it works

One piece of church land. Three contributors.

A central plot — ideally PCEA church land — hosts one shared zero-grazing enterprise. The church contributes land, members contribute capital, Mbuziplus contributes the livestock, housing and husbandry systems.

Church

Land and standing

Leases the site and lends its trust network to help recruit the founding cohort.

Members

Capital and labour

20–30 congregants fund the entry costs and share the ongoing running costs.

Mbuziplus

Livestock and systems

Supplies the goats, housing, husbandry protocol and links to buyers.

Together, these form one CZU — a single enterprise, jointly owned, run to a five-year growth plan.

Membership

Built for the farmer, and for the farmer at heart.

Not every congregant wants to show up and feed goats — and not every one can. Two tiers let the CZU take in both kinds of member without diluting either.

Tier one

On-site member

  • Lives near the unit and contributes labour toward feed, security and upkeep
  • Reduced cash contribution in exchange for hands-on work
  • First in line for napier and fodder-growing income
Tier two

Investor member

  • Urban or diaspora — capital only, no labour required
  • Pays the full pooled cash contribution and stays fully passive
  • Gets quarterly reporting on their unit, like a SACCO statement, for goats

Built to add a new cohort every year

The pilot proves the model at 20–30 members on one plot. Each following year can add a fresh cohort onto the same site's spare capacity, then onto additional church land as it becomes available.

Protection

How members' money — and the church's name — are protected.

Formal legal wrapper

Registered as an investment club or SACCO-style entity, with the church named as land partner.

Ring-fenced funds

Member contributions held by a named custodian, separate from any one person's control.

Insurance from year two

An annual premium covers herd loss once the pilot's first debts are serviced.

Defined lease term

Land agreement with the church set for a minimum of five years, with renewal terms agreed upfront.

Quarterly reporting

Every member, on-site or investor, receives a statement on their unit's health and output.

Defined exit terms

A clear process and timeline for any member who needs to exit before the five-year horizon.

Getting involved

What a CZU asks of a host church.

1

Lease of a central plot

The church retains ownership throughout; a defined lease term and a share of enterprise revenue return to it.

2

Endorsement to the congregation

Support in recruiting the first cohort of 20–30 members from within the church's own trust network.

3

A seat in governance

A formal role overseeing the enterprise, protecting both the church's name and its members' interests.
